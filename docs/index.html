Fruit-tree pruning desk

A field-guide desk for pruning fruit trees.

Crownwork teaches where to cut on apple, peach, and pear—with labeled anatomy, cut types (thinning vs heading, renewal, suckers, deadwood, narrow crotches, crossing wood), season timing, and what good vs bad cuts look like. Toggle Advanced for auxin, apical dominance, carbohydrate allocation, and CODIT wound response with Extension citations. Berries, vines, and other woody crops share the same shell.

Cut detail

Select a labeled marker on the tree to see how to make the cut, why it matters, when to do it, and what good vs bad looks like. Switch to Advanced for before-and-after physiology and sources.

  • ● Thinning — remove a limb at its origin; opens light without a flush of sprouts
  • ● Heading — shorten a shoot above a bud; thickens and branches
  • ● Renewal — replace aging fruiting wood with younger units
  • ● Sucker — remove watersprouts and root suckers at the origin
  • ● Deadwood / structural — sanitation, narrow crotches, crossing wood

Tree anatomy (field guide)

Labels on the diagram match this key. Learn the parts before you cut—fruiting wood and vegetative wood respond differently, and crotch angle predicts whether a scaffold will hold a crop.

Leader
The central vertical trunk/shoot that sets tree height and dominance (central-leader systems).
Scaffold
Primary permanent limb arising from the trunk; forms the framework.
Lateral
Side shoot off a scaffold or leader; may become fruiting wood or a secondary scaffold.
Spur
Short, stubby fruiting shoot (common on apple and pear) that carries flower buds for years.
Water sprout / sucker
Vigorous upright from branch or root; rarely good fruit wood—usually remove.
Crotch angle
Angle between trunk and scaffold. Wide (~45–60°) is strong; narrow with included bark is weak.
Fruiting wood
Spurs and laterals that set flower buds; needs light. Protect and renew it.
Vegetative wood
Extension shoots building structure or stealing vigor; manage with heading/thinning.
